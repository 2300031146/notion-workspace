## ✅ Task Breakdown

### Task 1: Financial Risk Inventory Analysis

**Objective:** Identify which products in Elevence's inventory pose a financial risk.

**Methodology:**
- Analyzed 1000+ products across the e-commerce platform
- Classified products as "risky" when: High discount % BUT low customer rating
- Calculated financial impact of risky products
- Mapped risk distribution across product categories

**Key Finding:**
🔴 **14.4% of total inventory value is at financial risk**

**Business Impact:**
- Identifies products that are being discounted but still receiving poor ratings
- Suggests these products need quality improvement, not just price reductions
- Actionable insight for product management and pricing teams
- Recommendations: Focus on quality improvement rather than aggressive discounting

**Deliverables:**
- Power BI Dashboard: `Financial risk inventory analysis.pbix`
- Detailed findings report with visualizations
- Risk categorization and recommendations

**Repository:** [`task-1-financial-risk`](https://github.com/2300031146/elevence-tasks/tree/main/task-1-financial-risk)

### Task 2: Discount vs Rating Causation Analysis

**Objective:** Determine whether higher discounts lead to better customer ratings.

**Methodology:**
- Segmented products into discount bands (0-10%, 10-20%, 20-30%, 30%+)
- Calculated average ratings for each discount band
- Analyzed correlation between discount percentage and customer satisfaction
- Performed statistical analysis to validate findings

**Key Finding:**
📊 **Higher discounts do NOT improve customer ratings**

- Ratings remain consistent at 3.5-4.5 across ALL discount levels
- Discounts attract more customers (increase volume/transaction count)
- BUT discounts do not improve actual product satisfaction or ratings
- Inverse correlation observed in some cases (deeper discounts → slightly lower ratings)

**Business Impact:**
- Pricing strategy should focus on product quality, not just discounts
- Discounts are effective for sales volume but NOT for customer satisfaction
- Challenges the common assumption: "Lower price = Higher rating"
- Implications: Quality improvements will have more impact on customer satisfaction than price reductions

**Deliverables:**
- Power BI Dashboard: `Discount_vs_Rating_Analysis.pbix`
- Comparative analysis charts showing rating distribution by discount band
- Statistical correlation analysis and findings report

**Repository:** [`task-2-discount-vs-rating`](https://github.com/2300031146/elevence-tasks/tree/main/task-2-discount-vs-rating)

---

### Task 3: Promotion Effectiveness Trend Analysis

**Objective:** Analyze how promotional campaigns affect customer engagement and satisfaction over time.

**Methodology:**
- Compared review volume during promotion periods vs normal periods
- Analyzed average ratings during promotion periods
- Tracked engagement metrics over time (daily/weekly trends)
- Identified promotion impact on product visibility and customer behavior

**Key Finding:**
📈 **Promotions increase review volume significantly BUT average ratings don't improve**

- Promotions drive 2-3x increase in review activity
- More customers engage during promotions
- BUT overall product rating doesn't increase
- Suggests: Promotions drive traffic and visibility, not quality perception

**Business Impact:**
- Promotions are effective for visibility and engagement metrics (KPIs)
- If rating improvement is the goal, focus on product quality instead
- Promotions should be timed strategically for volume targets, not satisfaction goals
- Recommendation: Use promotions to drive traffic; use quality improvements to drive ratings

**Deliverables:**
- Power BI Dashboard: `Promotion effectiveness trend analysis.pbix`
- Time-series analysis showing review trends during vs outside promotions
- Trend forecasting and recommendations

**Repository:** [`task-3-promotion-effectiveness`](https://github.com/2300031146/elevence-tasks/tree/main/task-3-promotion-effectiveness)
